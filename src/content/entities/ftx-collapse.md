Eleven days in November 2022 that turned effective altruism's brightest donor into one of history's great frauds.

**What happened.** On 2 November, CoinDesk published a leaked [Alameda Research](/e/alameda-research) balance sheet: most of its assets were FTT, a token [FTX](/e/ftx) itself had created. On 6 November [Changpeng Zhao](/e/changpeng-zhao) announced [Binance](/e/binance) would sell about $529 million of FTT, and a bank run began. On 8 November Bankman-Fried announced a Binance rescue; Zhao looked at the books and walked away. On 11 November FTX filed for bankruptcy, with roughly $8 billion of customer money missing. The [Future Fund](/e/ftx-future-fund) team resigned on 10 November, saying they had "fundamental questions about the legitimacy and integrity" of the business behind it.

**The question it leaves you with.** Was this a moral framework failing, or a man exploiting one? Your answer decides whether the lesson is "fix the ideas" or "fix the vetting".

**Where it goes in the story.** It [illustrates](/e/ends-justify-means) the ends-justify-means worry, and it tested EA's [warnings](/e/alameda-2018-split): Alameda staff had told EA leaders in 2018 that Bankman-Fried was dishonest. It led to [US v. Bankman-Fried](/e/us-v-bankman-fried), 25 years in prison, and an ironic coda: FTX's $500M stake in [Anthropic](/e/anthropic), [sold](/e/ftx-anthropic-stake-sale) for about $1.3 billion in 2024, helped repay the defrauded customers.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Defenders say one fraudster no more discredits EA than Madoff discredits investing. Critics reply that EA didn't merely have a fraudster among its members; it held him up as the model of its strategy, and its institutions took his money after being warned.
