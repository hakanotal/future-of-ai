[Hilary Greaves](/e/hilary-greaves) and [William MacAskill](/e/william-macaskill)'s 2021 working paper at Oxford's [Global Priorities Institute](/e/global-priorities-institute): the academic backbone of [longtermism](/e/longtermism).

The thesis: in a wide class of decisions, the best option is the one whose effects on the very long-run future are best. The numbers do the work. The authors argue that "any reasonable estimate of the expected number of future beings is at least 10^24". Even if there were "only" 10^14, cutting near-term extinction risk by one millionth of one percentage point would be worth a million lives; on the main estimate, "ten quadrillion (10^16) lives saved".

The paper is more careful than its critics sometimes allow. It admits the case could look "driven purely by tiny credences in highly speculative scenarios" and tests it against far smaller numbers ([fanaticism](/e/fanaticism)).

**A question to take away:** if the conclusion holds even at the small numbers, is anything in the present left that could outweigh it?
