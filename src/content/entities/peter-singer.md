Australian philosopher (born 1946), and the intellectual grandfather of [effective altruism](/e/effective-altruism). In 1971, aged 25 and working at Oxford, he wrote [Famine, Affluence, and Morality](/e/famine-affluence-and-morality), which contains the [drowning child](/e/drowning-child). He later wrote *Animal Liberation* (1975) and [The Life You Can Save](/e/the-life-you-can-save) (2009), and taught at Princeton from 1999 until 2023.

Admirers call him one of the most influential living philosophers; critics, especially in disability-rights movements, have called him dangerous for his views on infanticide and severe disability. Both sides agree his logic is hard to escape. That is what makes him central here: he showed that a few plain premises, followed honestly, can overturn ordinary life. The movement he inspired inherited both the power of that move and its unanswered questions about *whose* suffering counts, *when*, and *who decides*.
