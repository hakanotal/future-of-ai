Charity evaluator founded in 2007 by [Holden Karnofsky](/e/holden-karnofsky) and [Elie Hassenfeld](/e/elie-hassenfeld), two analysts who left the hedge fund Bridgewater Associates after finding that charities could not tell them what their donations achieved. It judges charities not by overhead ratios or glossy reports but by evidence of outcomes and cost per life saved.

Its recommendations are deliberately unglamorous: bed nets from the [Against Malaria Foundation](/e/against-malaria-foundation), vitamin A supplements, cash incentives for childhood vaccination, programs run by groups like [Evidence Action](/e/evidence-action). By its own heavily caveated estimates, a few thousand dollars to its top charities averts a child's death. This is the part of the story that is hardest to argue with: [Singer's pond](/e/drowning-child) turned into a working mechanism. It is also where the critique from the receiving end lands. [Anthony Kalulu](/e/anthony-kalulu) points out that its top charities are Western-led organizations working in Africa, not African-led ones, and asks who gets to define effectiveness.
