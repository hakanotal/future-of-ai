The belief that humanity's destiny is to leave Earth, spread through the cosmos and remake it. It is the bundle's oldest strand and its most openly religious.

**The idea.** It begins with [Nikolai Fedorov](/e/nikolai-fedorov), a Moscow librarian of the late nineteenth century who taught that humanity's "common task" was to defeat death, physically resurrect every ancestor who had ever lived, and settle the stars to make room for them. In 2010 the AI researcher [Ben Goertzel](/e/ben-goertzel) revived the word for "modern cosmism" in his [Cosmist Manifesto](/e/cosmist-manifesto), which promises that "space-time engineering and future magic will permit achieving by scientific means most of the promises of religions", including resurrecting the dead "by copying them to the future".

**The question it leaves you with.** If humanity could live among the stars, would that justify neglecting the planet we are on now? Is "we will expand" an answer to "we are running out", or a way of not answering it?

**Where it goes in the story.** [Elon Musk](/e/elon-musk)'s plan to make life multiplanetary through [SpaceX](/e/spacex) is cosmism's most visible contemporary project, and the cosmic endowment is the source of longtermism's largest numbers: [Bostrom](/e/nick-bostrom)'s [astronomical waste](/e/astronomical-waste) counts the lives lost for every century we delay settling the universe.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Almost nobody calls themselves a cosmist today, so critics of TESCREAL are accused of inventing a strand. The reply is that the *idea* of limitless expansion is everywhere in the industry, even where the label is not, and it does quiet work: it promises unlimited growth in a finite world by redefining the size of the container.
