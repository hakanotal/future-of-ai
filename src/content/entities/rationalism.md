Not the rationalism of Descartes. This is the internet community built around [Eliezer Yudkowsky](/e/eliezer-yudkowsky): first [Overcoming Bias](/e/overcoming-bias) (2006), then [LessWrong](/e/lesswrong) (2009) and Scott Alexander's [Slate Star Codex](/e/slate-star-codex) (2013).

**The idea.** Humans are riddled with cognitive biases; with practice, Bayesian reasoning and decision theory, you can think more clearly and act better. The on-ramps were Yudkowsky's [Sequences](/e/sequences) and his fanfiction [Harry Potter and the Methods of Rationality](/e/hpmor), through which, a 2013 survey found, about a quarter of LessWrong users arrived. The community's defining obsession became AI: if minds can be improved, a mind much smarter than ours could be built, and it might not share our values.

**The question it leaves you with.** Can you reason your way to a conclusion so strange that your own horror at it counts as a bias to overcome? In [Torture vs. Dust Specks](/e/torture-vs-dust-specks), Yudkowsky argues that fifty years of torture for one person is better than a momentary dust speck in the eye for an unimaginable number of people. Where would you stop the chain of logic, and why there?

**Where it goes in the story.** Rationalism is where [AI alignment](/e/ai-alignment) and [AI doomerism](/e/doomerism) came from, and, per Sam Altman, where the founders of OpenAI and DeepMind first got interested in AGI. It overlaps heavily with [effective altruism](/e/effective-altruism). It has also drawn serious criticism: [Gideon Lewis-Kraus](/e/gideon-lewis-kraus) says it "gave safe harbour to some genuinely egregious ideas", including neoreaction ([Curtis Yarvin](/e/curtis-yarvin)); TIME and Bloomberg reported misconduct against women in rationalist and EA circles; and the violent [Zizians](/e/zizians) grew out of its Bay Area fringe.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Most rationalists reject Yudkowsky's doom, and "some members did terrible things" is not an argument against Bayes' theorem. True. But a movement that claims to be unusually good at reasoning makes its reasoning failures fair game.
