The belief that a technological singularity is coming, a point after which progress is so fast that unaugmented humans can no longer follow it, and that we should hurry it along.

**The idea.** [Émile P. Torres](/e/emile-torres) separates two versions. One is about *rate*: imagine the change between 1925 and today compressed into a minute, then a second. The other is the [intelligence explosion](/e/intelligence-explosion): build an AI as good as a human at AI research, let it design its successor, and AGI becomes AGI+ becomes AGI++ within days or hours. [Ray Kurzweil](/e/ray-kurzweil) made the first version famous in [The Singularity Is Near](/e/singularity-is-near) (2005): human-level AI by 2029, a merger of humans and machines around 2045.

**The question it leaves you with.** If you believed the most important event in history might happen within ten years, what would you be willing to skip, postpone or sacrifice to be ready for it? And what would you stop noticing?

**Where it goes in the story.** Singularity timelines are no longer fringe. The 2025 scenario [AI 2027](/e/ai-2027) and Leopold Aschenbrenner's [Situational Awareness](/e/situational-awareness) turned them into forecasts about labs, chips and China, and that framing fed straight into the [AGI race](/e/ai-race) and the recasting of AI as a [security](/e/ai-security) problem. The scholar [Robert Geraci](/e/robert-geraci) reads the whole genre as [apocalyptic theology](/e/techno-millenarianism): a fallen present, a coming rupture, a digital afterlife.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Capabilities really did jump in the 2020s, so "they said it would happen and it is happening" is the believers' best argument. The sceptic's reply: a forecast that sets the budget, the regulation and the geopolitics can make itself come true without being correct about the physics.
