A movement built on one question: if you want to do good, how do you do *the most* good? It grew out of [Peter Singer](/e/peter-singer)'s [drowning child](/e/drowning-child) and was organized at Oxford by [Toby Ord](/e/toby-ord) and [William MacAskill](/e/william-macaskill) through [Giving What We Can](/e/give-what-we-can) (2009), [80,000 Hours](/e/eighty-thousand-hours) (2011) and the [Centre for Effective Altruism](/e/centre-for-effective-altruism) (2012), alongside [GiveWell](/e/givewell) and [Open Philanthropy](/e/open-philanthropy) in the US.

**The idea.** Charities differ in effectiveness by orders of magnitude, so evidence and arithmetic matter more than good intentions. The method rests on [impartiality](/e/impartiality) (everyone counts equally), [welfarism](/e/welfarism) (well-being is what counts) and [expected utility](/e/expected-utility) (multiply value by probability and maximize). A concrete result: a few thousand dollars to the [Against Malaria Foundation](/e/against-malaria-foundation) buys enough bed nets, by GiveWell's estimates, to save a child's life.

**The question it leaves you with.** "Do the most good" hides three smaller questions. Good *for whom*? Over *what time horizon*? And *decided by whom*? Each answer EA gave reshaped the movement.

**Where it goes in the story.** Answer "for whom" with "everyone, including the unborn" and you get [longtermism](/e/longtermism). Answer "how" with "earn money" and you get [earning to give](/e/earning-to-give) and, eventually, [Sam Bankman-Fried](/e/sam-bankman-fried). Answer "which risks" with "AI" and you get EA's biggest legacy: the people and money that built [AI safety](/e/ai-safety), from [Anthropic](/e/anthropic)'s founders to the Washington think tank [CSET](/e/cset). After the [FTX collapse](/e/ftx-collapse), the CEO of the Centre for Effective Altruism wrote in March 2025 that EA had spent two years "in a defensive crouch"; by early 2026 CEA was reporting growth again.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** From the receiving end, [Anthony Kalulu](/e/anthony-kalulu) argues that EA pushes "predetermined solutions" not rooted in the lives of the poor. [Amia Srinivasan](/e/amia-srinivasan) says it promises a better world while encouraging us to accept this one "more or less as it is". The defenders' answer deserves stating in full: bed nets work, vitamin A works, cash works, and "fund grassroots organizing instead" is harder to measure. That is the disagreement, not a rebuttal of it.
