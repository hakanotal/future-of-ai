Scottish philosopher (born 1987) and the central organizer of [effective altruism](/e/effective-altruism). He co-founded [Giving What We Can](/e/give-what-we-can) (2009), [80,000 Hours](/e/eighty-thousand-hours) (2011) and the [Centre for Effective Altruism](/e/centre-for-effective-altruism) (2012), and wrote [Doing Good Better](/e/doing-good-better) (2015) and [What We Owe the Future](/e/what-we-owe-the-future) (2022). With [Toby Ord](/e/toby-ord) he coined [longtermism](/e/longtermism); with [Hilary Greaves](/e/hilary-greaves) he wrote [its academic case](/e/strong-longtermism-2021).

His arc runs through every part of this story. He pitched [earning to give](/e/earning-to-give) to students, including, around 2012, [Sam Bankman-Fried](/e/sam-bankman-fried). He was warned about Bankman-Fried in 2018 ([Alameda split](/e/alameda-2018-split)) and advised the [FTX Future Fund](/e/ftx-future-fund) in 2022. After the collapse he wrote: "If that goodwill laundered fraud, I am ashamed." In later reflections he warned against assuming that people who display strong moral commitment, by giving a lot or being vegan, must have integrity in other areas too.

He is, notably, among the *moderate* longtermists. That is why critics argue about what the framework is for, not only about him.
