The grantmaker, funded mainly by [Dustin Moskovitz](/e/dustin-moskovitz) and [Cari Tuna](/e/cari-tuna) through [Good Ventures](/e/good-ventures), that grew out of GiveWell in 2014 under [Holden Karnofsky](/e/holden-karnofsky). It has directed more than $4 billion. On 18 November 2025 it renamed itself Coefficient Giving and opened its funds to other donors.

It is the single most important funder of the AI-safety field, and so one of the main answers to [who decides](/e/ai-governance). Three grants show the range. In 2017 it gave [OpenAI](/e/openai) $30 million, with a board seat for Karnofsky. In 2019 it gave $55 million to launch Georgetown's [CSET](/e/cset), which helped build Washington's thinking on [AI security](/e/ai-security). And it has funded alignment research, [MIRI](/e/machine-intelligence-research-institute), the [Global Priorities Institute](/e/global-priorities-institute) and much of EA's infrastructure, alongside large global-health and animal-welfare portfolios. None of this was secret. But nobody voted for it either: in philanthropy, the research agenda is set by whoever pays.
