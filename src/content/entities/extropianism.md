The first organized transhumanist movement, launched by the philosopher [Max More](/e/max-more) in 1988 through *Extropy* magazine and, from 1992, the [Extropy Institute](/e/extropy-institute).

**The idea.** Entropy is the universe's tendency to fall apart; *extropy* is the opposite, the growth of order, intelligence and life. Extropians treated death as a technical problem, denounced "deathism", and practised what they preached through cryonics, freezing their bodies at [Alcor](/e/alcor) in the hope of future revival. The organizer and podcaster [Kelly Hayes](/e/kelly-hayes) calls it pro-science super-optimism at war with entropy.

**The question it leaves you with.** If death could be abolished, should it be? And if only some people could afford not to die, what would that do to everyone else's lives?

**Where it goes in the story.** The movement is gone, but its mailing list was a seedbed. [Nick Bostrom](/e/nick-bostrom), [Robin Hanson](/e/robin-hanson) and a teenage [Eliezer Yudkowsky](/e/eliezer-yudkowsky) all posted there; [Ray Kurzweil](/e/ray-kurzweil) and [Marvin Minsky](/e/marvin-minsky) moved in the same milieu. Yudkowsky's [Singularity Institute](/e/singularity-institute) was bankrolled by extropian entrepreneurs. And the 1996 racist email Bostrom [apologized for](/e/bostrom-email-apology) in 2023 was posted to the Extropians list, which is why critics read it as a clue to the genealogy rather than a private lapse.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** A 1990s mailing list is not a party line, and plenty of its members went on to disagree with each other about almost everything. The reply: that is exactly the TESCREAL point. The ideas were argued over, not agreed on, but the people, the vocabulary and the funders carried forward.
