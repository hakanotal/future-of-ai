The longtermist claim that the present is unusually, perhaps uniquely, influential: that decisions made in this century will shape the long-term future far more than decisions made in most others.

**The idea.** If powerful AI, engineered pandemics or [value lock-in](/e/value-lock-in) could fix humanity's trajectory soon, then we are standing on the hinge, and the resources of the present should go to getting it right. [Ord](/e/toby-ord)'s *Precipice* is the same image: a narrow ledge between a dangerous present and a secure future.

**The question it leaves you with.** Every generation has believed it lived at a turning point. How would you tell whether ours actually is? And who benefits from the belief that it is?

**Where it goes in the story.** The hinge is what turns longtermism from a philosophy into a budget. It justifies redirecting money from today's emergencies (the [far-future priority objection](/e/far-future-priority-objection)) and, in its security version, a [race](/e/ai-race) to get there first.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Some longtermists, including MacAskill in earlier work, have argued we are probably *not* at the most influential time, and should save resources for later. Critics note the hinge claim has religious form: the end is near, and the faithful must act ([techno-millenarianism](/e/techno-millenarianism)).
