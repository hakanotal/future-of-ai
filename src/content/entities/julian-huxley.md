British biologist (1887–1975), first director-general of UNESCO, and the man who gave [transhumanism](/e/transhumanism) its name. In [New Bottles for New Wine](/e/new-bottles-for-new-wine) (1957) he wrote that "the human species can, if it wishes, transcend itself… in its entirety, as humanity."

He was also a committed eugenicist and president of the British Eugenics Society from 1959 to 1962. He argued that deliberate "artificial selection" could "operate much more rapidly and much less wastefully than natural selection." That pairing, the founder of transhumanism and a leader of [eugenics](/e/eugenics), is the linchpin of the [TESCREAL](/e/tescreal) genealogy argument. Transhumanists reply that ideas are not responsible for their founders. Critics reply that the question "who decides what counts as improvement?" came with the name.
