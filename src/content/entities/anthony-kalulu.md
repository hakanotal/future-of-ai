Farmer and community organizer from Busoga, Uganda's poorest region, and the author of one of the most searching critiques of [effective altruism](/e/effective-altruism) from the receiving end. Kalulu argues that traditional philanthropy and EA alike push "their own predetermined solutions, which aren't rooted in the lived experiences of the extreme poor", that such solutions don't last, and that only about 1% of global anti-poverty funding reaches grassroots organizations in the Global South. The proposed alternative is to fund local groups that understand root causes. The critique raises the governance question in miniature: not whether aid works, but who decides what help looks like.
