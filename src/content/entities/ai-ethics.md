The study of AI's present-day harms: biased systems, surveillance, stolen data and creative work, exploited labor, environmental cost, concentrated power. Its home institutions, like [DAIR](/e/dair) and the [AI Now Institute](/e/ai-now-institute), have run on a fraction of the money that flowed to [AI safety](/e/ai-safety).

**The idea.** Harm is already here and falls on particular people. The founding text for many is [On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots](/e/stochastic-parrots) (2021), by [Emily Bender](/e/emily-bender), [Timnit Gebru](/e/timnit-gebru), Angelina McMillan-Major and [Margaret Mitchell](/e/margaret-mitchell): large language models are costly to train, encode the biases of their data, and give an illusion of understanding. A concrete case: in 2023 TIME reported that Kenyan workers at [Sama](/e/sama) labeled horrific content for OpenAI for under $2 an hour, work that made the chatbot "safe" to use.

**The question it leaves you with.** If a system is safe from ending the world but depends on traumatized, underpaid workers and a city's drinking water, is it safe? Who gets to answer that?

**Where it goes in the story.** Google forced Gebru out in December 2020 in the [dispute over that paper](/e/gebru-google-exit). She then co-coined [TESCREAL](/e/tescreal) to explain why the industry funded speculative catastrophe while treating present harms as a distraction. The [EU AI Act](/e/eu-ai-act), with its focus on rights and risk tiers, is the largest attempt to write AI ethics into law.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Safety researchers argue that the two agendas need not compete, and that catastrophic misuse (bioweapons, cyberattacks) is a present harm too. Ethics researchers reply that they would welcome the merger, once the funding, the seats at the table and the definition of "risk" are shared.
