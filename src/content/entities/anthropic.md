AI company founded in 2021 by seven former [OpenAI](/e/openai) staff: [Dario](/e/dario-amodei) and [Daniela Amodei](/e/daniela-amodei), [Jared Kaplan](/e/jared-kaplan), [Sam McCandlish](/e/sam-mccandlish), [Tom Brown](/e/tom-brown), [Chris Olah](/e/chris-olah) and [Jack Clark](/e/jack-clark), with [Ben Mann](/e/ben-mann) in the early team. They left over speed versus safety. (Disclosure: this site's revision was assisted by Claude, an Anthropic model.)

Anthropic is the most direct institutional descendant of EA's AI-safety concerns, and the best test of whether those concerns survive contact with the market. It is a public benefit corporation with a [Long-Term Benefit Trust](/e/long-term-benefit-trust) and a [Responsible Scaling Policy](/e/responsible-scaling-policy). Its early funding included $500 million from [FTX](/e/ftx), later traced to misused customer money, and billions from [Amazon](/e/amazon) and [Google](/e/google). [Holden Karnofsky](/e/holden-karnofsky) joined in 2025.

Critics say it is the [race](/e/ai-race) with better manners: building the thing it warns about. Supporters say someone careful has to be at the frontier. In 2026 its refusal to allow fully autonomous weapons or domestic mass surveillance led to the [Pentagon dispute](/e/anthropic-pentagon-dispute): a company's safety lines against a state's security claims. Either way, it shows that [who decides](/e/ai-governance) is no longer an abstract question.
