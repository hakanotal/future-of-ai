Oxford philosopher whose foreword to [The Good It Promises, the Harm It Does](/e/good-it-promises-harm-it-does) (2023) is the most-quoted short critique of [effective altruism](/e/effective-altruism). She notes that EA has found almost nothing to learn from historic movements for the rights of workers, colonized peoples, women, disabled and incarcerated people, treating them at best as *ineffective* ways of doing good rather than "political formations from which to learn". Her verdict: EA "promises us a better world while implicitly encouraging us to accept the world more or less as it is."
