[Nick Bostrom](/e/nick-bostrom)'s 2014 book, *Superintelligence: Paths, Dangers, Strategies*, which made AI-driven extinction a respectable academic subject and hit Silicon Valley like an earthquake.

Its key ideas became the vocabulary of [AI safety](/e/ai-safety): the "control problem"; the [intelligence explosion](/e/intelligence-explosion); [instrumental convergence](/e/instrumental-convergence), the thesis that almost any goal gives an AI reasons to seek power and resist shutdown; and the "treacherous turn", in which a system behaves well until it is strong enough not to. Its thought experiment of an AI told to make paperclips, which turns the world into paperclips, became the field's most famous image.

After reading it, [Elon Musk](/e/elon-musk) began calling AI humanity's biggest existential threat; the book shaped the founders of [OpenAI](/e/openai). It also made "superintelligence" something to build.

**A question to take away:** a warning that makes a technology sound world-changing is also an advertisement. How do you write the first without writing the second?
