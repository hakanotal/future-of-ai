Seven long words, one acronym: [Transhumanism](/e/transhumanism), [Extropianism](/e/extropianism), [Singularitarianism](/e/singularitarianism), [Cosmism](/e/cosmism), [Rationalism](/e/rationalism), [Effective Altruism](/e/effective-altruism) and [Longtermism](/e/longtermism). [Timnit Gebru](/e/timnit-gebru) and [Émile P. Torres](/e/emile-torres) coined it in 2023 and set out the case in [The TESCREAL Bundle](/e/tescreal-bundle-paper) (*First Monday*, April 2024).

**The idea.** These look like separate schools, some of them openly hostile to each other. Gebru and Torres argue they are better read as one family, with three things in common: a shared genealogy running back to twentieth-century [eugenics](/e/eugenics), overlapping people and money, and a common hope that technology, above all artificial general intelligence, will carry humanity to a radically "improved" or [posthuman](/e/posthuman) future. A concrete test: [Nick Bostrom](/e/nick-bostrom) posted to the Extropians mailing list in the 1990s, founded the institute that incubated longtermism, and wrote the book that made [Elon Musk](/e/elon-musk) fear AI. One career, several letters.

**The question it leaves you with.** If two people disagree bitterly about whether AI will save or destroy us, but agree that it is the most important thing in history and that people like them should be the ones steering it, do they disagree about much?

**Where it goes in the story.** The bundle explains why the fiercest AI debate of the 2020s, [doomers](/e/doomerism) against [accelerationists](/e/effective-accelerationism), was fought mostly between people who read the same blogs and took money from the same funders. It also explains what that debate kept leaving out: the present-day harms tracked by [AI ethics](/e/ai-ethics), and the question of [who decides](/e/ai-governance).

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Critics such as [Seth Lazar](/e/seth-lazar), [Ethan Zuckerman](/e/ethan-zuckerman), [Ozy Brennan](/e/ozy-brennan) and [Oliver Habryka](/e/oliver-habryka) say the acronym turns rivals into a monolith and a polemic into history ("I've never in my life met a cosmist," Habryka wrote). The fair reply is to use TESCREAL as a map of genealogy and funding networks, not an accusation that anyone believes all seven things. Read that way, it is the most useful single word for the milieu that built the AI industry. Read as a conspiracy, it stops being useful at all.
