"The TESCREAL Bundle: Eugenics and the Promise of Utopia through Artificial General Intelligence", by [Timnit Gebru](/e/timnit-gebru) and [Émile P. Torres](/e/emile-torres), published in *First Monday* in April 2024 after the term had circulated in drafts and talks since 2023.

The paper makes a genealogical argument. The seven ideologies of [TESCREAL](/e/tescreal) descend from twentieth-century Anglo-American [eugenics](/e/eugenics), share key personnel and funders, and share a utopian hope that AGI will deliver a radically "improved" humanity. That hope, the authors argue, drives the [AGI race](/e/ai-race), legitimizes building unscoped and dangerous systems, and pushes present harms out of view.

Its most common misreading is that it alleges a single coordinated conspiracy. It doesn't: it claims shared roots, people and logic, not shared belief. Its most substantive critics ([Lazar](/e/seth-lazar), [Zuckerman](/e/ethan-zuckerman), [Brennan](/e/ozy-brennan)) argue that even the genealogy flattens important differences.

**A question to take away:** can you trace an idea's ancestry without making its current holders answer for their ancestors? When is genealogy an insight, and when is it guilt by association?
