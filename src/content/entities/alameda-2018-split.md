In 2018, about half of Alameda Research's early staff walked out, and several of them tried to warn the leaders of effective altruism about the man they were leaving.

**What happened.** According to TIME's March 2023 investigation by [Charlotte Alter](/e/charlotte-alter), departing staff, including co-founder [Tara Mac Aulay](/e/tara-mac-aulay), a former senior executive at the Centre for Effective Altruism, told EA figures that Bankman-Fried was dishonest, negligent, mixed personal and company money, and refused basic business controls. An internal document accused him of "gross negligence" and said: "Sam will lie, and distort the truth for his own gain." The warnings reached [William MacAskill](/e/william-macaskill), [Nick Beckstead](/e/nick-beckstead) and [Holden Karnofsky](/e/holden-karnofsky). Engineer [Naia Bouscal](/e/naia-bouscal) said MacAskill "basically threatened" those raising concerns; others described the concerns being dismissed as a "he said-she said" dispute.

**The question it leaves you with.** A movement that prided itself on weighing evidence received evidence about its most important donor. What made it easier to discount than a spreadsheet about bed nets?

**Where it goes in the story.** Four years later Bankman-Fried was funding EA through the [Future Fund](/e/ftx-future-fund), led by Beckstead with MacAskill advising. After the [collapse](/e/ftx-collapse), MacAskill wrote: "If that goodwill laundered fraud, I am ashamed."

**Strongest objection / best reply.** EA leaders say the warnings concerned management and ethics, not fraud, and that no one foresaw customer funds being stolen. Critics answer that "lies for his own gain" is not a management issue.
