On 28 October 2025, OpenAI finished turning its for-profit arm into a public benefit corporation, closing the argument that began when its nonprofit board fired and lost Sam Altman two years earlier.

**What happened.** The nonprofit became the OpenAI Foundation; the company became OpenAI Group PBC. The Foundation keeps control and a stake of about 26%; [Microsoft](/e/microsoft) holds about 27%. OpenAI's original plan would have removed the nonprofit's oversight altogether; after pressure from the attorneys general of Delaware and California, it kept nonprofit control, and Delaware issued a "statement of no objection". [Elon Musk](/e/elon-musk), a co-founder, sued to stop the change.

**The question it leaves you with.** OpenAI's charter says its mission is to ensure AGI "benefits all of humanity". In the end, the decision about how to protect that mission was reviewed by two state officials and negotiated with investors. Who represented "all of humanity" in the room?

**Where it goes in the story.** It shows [industry self-governance](/e/industry-self-governance) being partly absorbed by ordinary corporate law, with state regulators as the backstop. Compare the two main labs: OpenAI keeps a foundation in control; [Anthropic](/e/anthropic) relies on its [Long-Term Benefit Trust](/e/long-term-benefit-trust).

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Supporters say the new structure is more honest about the capital frontier AI needs, while still binding the company to its mission. Critics say a public-benefit label is only as strong as whoever enforces it, and nobody yet knows who that is.
