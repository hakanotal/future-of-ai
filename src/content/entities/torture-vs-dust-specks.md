[Eliezer Yudkowsky](/e/eliezer-yudkowsky)'s 2007 LessWrong post, and the rationalist thought experiment critics cite most often.

**The idea.** Would you rather one person be tortured for fifty years, or 3^^^3 people (a number, written in Knuth's up-arrow notation, too large to describe) each get a barely noticeable dust speck in the eye? Yudkowsky argues the specks are worse: add up enough tiny harms and they outweigh any single harm. That is [expected utility](/e/expected-utility) and aggregation taken to their limit.

**The question it leaves you with.** Which do you choose, and why? If you choose the specks, what principle are you using, and does it survive being written down? If you choose the torture, is there any aggregation you would refuse?

**Where it goes in the story.** To critics, the post is the whole method in miniature: everything reduces to numbers, nothing is simply wrong, and a conclusion almost everyone finds monstrous is presented as a test of nerve. The same reasoning shape appears in [longtermism](/e/longtermism)'s 10^16 lives and in the [public-defender](/e/replaceability) anecdote.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Defenders say the thought experiment is deliberately extreme so as to isolate a principle, and that philosophers have long used cases like it. Critics answer that philosophy seminars don't usually run billion-dollar foundations or AI labs.
