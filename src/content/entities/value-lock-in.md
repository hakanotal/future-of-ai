[William MacAskill](/e/william-macaskill)'s warning in [What We Owe the Future](/e/what-we-owe-the-future): some set of values could become permanently entrenched, through a world government, a dominant ideology or, above all, AGI, and freeze moral progress for millennia.

**The idea.** History's values have been contingent: abolition was not inevitable. If a powerful AI is built to carry today's values, or its owner's, those could become the last values that ever change anything. That is why, on this view, the present is the [hinge of history](/e/hinge-of-history).

**The question it leaves you with.** Which of your current moral beliefs would you be comfortable locking in forever? Which of your great-grandparents' beliefs would you have wanted locked in?

**Where it goes in the story.** Lock-in is the longtermist argument that comes closest to the critics' concerns. Both sides fear a small group permanently deciding for everyone. They differ on who that group is. For MacAskill it is whoever controls AGI first; for [Gebru and Torres](/e/tescreal) it may be the TESCREAL milieu itself. Ord's proposed antidote is the [Long Reflection](/e/long-reflection): once safe, deliberate for a long time before choosing.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Critics ask who would run a "long reflection", and who would be excluded. Longtermists reply that the whole point is to keep options open. The shared insight is worth holding on to: [who decides](/e/ai-governance) may matter more than what gets decided first.
