A word coined by the novelist [Margaret Atwood](/e/margaret-atwood) in 2011 and taken up by the sociologist [Ruha Benjamin](/e/ruha-benjamin): "Utopias are the stuff of dreams, dystopias the stuff of nightmares; ustopias are what we create together when we're wide awake."

**The idea.** Benjamin argues that techno-utopia and techno-apocalypse are two sides of one coin ([the binary](/e/utopia-apocalypse-binary)): in both, technology acts *on* us, and the human inputs, the values, interests and needs that shape it, go missing. "To move forward, we have to pull back the screen." A small sliver of humanity invests in space travel, superintelligence and bunkers while casting healthcare and housing for all as unimaginable. Their imaginations run wild about bending physical and digital reality, she says, but "grow limp" at transforming social reality.

**The question it leaves you with.** Why is a Mars colony easier to picture than housing for everyone? The activist [Rob Hopkins](/e/rob-hopkins) calls this the [crisis of imagination](/e/crisis-of-imagination). What would it take for you to imagine the second as vividly as the first?

**Where it goes in the story.** Ustopia is the democratic answer to the question of [who decides](/e/ai-governance). There are working examples. [Decidim](/e/decidim), the open-source participatory platform, is used by Barcelona, Helsinki and New York. [DAIR](/e/dair)'s *Possible Futures* series imagines technology grown from community needs, like [Nyalleng Moorosi](/e/nyalleng-moorosi)'s tools for informal food-trading networks.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Sceptics say participatory democracy is slow, and frontier AI is fast and technical. The reply: every "fast" decision in this story (a board firing, a funding round, a rename, a Pentagon ultimatum) was made by very few people. Speed is not neutral; it decides who gets to be in the room.
