The position that genetic and technological enhancement becomes acceptable when it is chosen freely by individuals and parents rather than imposed by states. It is the transhumanist answer to the history of [eugenics](/e/eugenics), and it is defended in papers like [Defending Eugenics](/e/defending-eugenics). The break with coercion is real. The critics' question is whether markets and inequality can quietly recreate the old sorting: when only some can afford the embryo screen or the implant, "choice" decides who is improved.
