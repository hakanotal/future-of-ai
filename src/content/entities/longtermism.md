The view that positively influencing the long-term future is *the* key moral priority of our time. The word was coined around 2017 by [Toby Ord](/e/toby-ord) and [William MacAskill](/e/william-macaskill).

**The idea.** Three premises, as MacAskill put them in [What We Owe the Future](/e/what-we-owe-the-future) (2022): future people matter as much as present people; there could be an enormous number of them; and what we do now can change whether they exist and how they live. It extends Singer's [impartiality](/e/impartiality) from space to time, draws on [Derek Parfit](/e/derek-parfit)'s [non-identity problem](/e/non-identity-problem), and runs on [expected utility](/e/expected-utility). The arithmetic in [The Case for Strong Longtermism](/e/strong-longtermism-2021): if there are at least 10^24 expected future beings, cutting extinction risk by one millionth of one percentage point is worth ten quadrillion (10^16) lives.

**The question it leaves you with.** What could someone in the year 1500 have done to improve the world of 2026? If the answer is "almost nothing we could have predicted", how confident should we be about the year 3000?

**Where it goes in the story.** Longtermism turned EA's attention from today's poor to [existential risk](/e/existential-risk), and above all to AI. It supplied the moral case for the [FTX Future Fund](/e/ftx-future-fund), for alignment research, and for the claim that we live at the [hinge of history](/e/hinge-of-history). It also supplied the numbers that make critics most uneasy: against 10^16 lives, a billion people saved from famine is, literally, a rounding error.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Four objections recur: the future is unpredictable; present emergencies get crowded out ([Hupfer](/e/elizabeth-hupfer)); astronomical stakes can license [any means](/e/ends-justify-means); and the genealogy leads back to [eugenics](/e/eugenics) ([Torres](/e/emile-torres)). Philosophers add [fanaticism](/e/fanaticism): tiny probabilities of vast payoffs should not swamp everything else. Longtermists reply that most of their actual work (pandemic preparedness, AI safety) also helps people alive today, and that caring about the future should not be controversial. The live question is whether the moderate practice can be separated from the maximal arithmetic.
