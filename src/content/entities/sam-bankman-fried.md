Founder of [Alameda Research](/e/alameda-research) (2017) and [FTX](/e/ftx) (2019), once effective altruism's model citizen and now serving a 25-year sentence for fraud.

The son of two Stanford law professors, he studied physics at MIT, where around 2012 [William MacAskill](/e/william-macaskill) persuaded him that [earning to give](/e/earning-to-give) would do more good than working in animal welfare. He traded at [Jane Street](/e/jane-street), worked briefly at the [Centre for Effective Altruism](/e/centre-for-effective-altruism), then built a crypto empire worth, at its peak, about $26 billion on paper. He funded the [FTX Future Fund](/e/ftx-future-fund), political campaigns, and a $500M stake in [Anthropic](/e/anthropic).

After the [collapse](/e/ftx-collapse) he told Vox that his "ethics stuff" was "mostly a front… a dumb game we woke westerners play where we say all the right shibboleths and so everyone likes us" ([the DMs](/e/sbf-vox-interview)). He was convicted on seven counts in November 2023. Was he a sincere [ends-justify-means](/e/ends-justify-means) utilitarian, or a cynic wearing the costume? The answer changes the lesson, and the evidence points both ways.
