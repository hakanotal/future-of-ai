Self-taught AI theorist (born 1979), founder of [rationalism](/e/rationalism) and the most influential [AI doomer](/e/doomerism).

As a teenage [extropian](/e/extropianism) he expected superintelligence to be wonderful and imminent. Extropian donors funded his [Singularity Institute](/e/singularity-institute) (2000) to build it. Within a few years he concluded that smart does not mean good, and spent the rest of his career on [alignment](/e/ai-alignment). He co-founded [Overcoming Bias](/e/overcoming-bias) and [LessWrong](/e/lesswrong), wrote the [Sequences](/e/sequences) and [HPMOR](/e/hpmor), and helped organize the [Singularity Summit](/e/singularity-summit), where DeepMind's founders met Peter Thiel.

The irony is the story's hinge. [Sam Altman](/e/sam-altman) wrote in 2023 that Yudkowsky "has IMO done more to accelerate AGI than anyone else": he "got many of us interested in AGI, helped deepmind get funded… was critical in the decision to start openai". In the same year Yudkowsky [wrote in TIME](/e/yudkowsky-time-oped-2023) that building superhuman AI "under anything remotely like the current circumstances" would kill everyone. With [Nate Soares](/e/nate-soares) he restated it in [If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies](/e/if-anyone-builds-it-everyone-dies) (2025). His life poses a hard question: what does it take for a warning not to become a recruitment pitch?
