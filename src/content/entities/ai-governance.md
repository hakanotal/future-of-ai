The question this whole story has been circling: who decides what "safe" and "secure" AI means, by what process, and on whose behalf?

**The idea.** Every group in this story has offered an answer. *The labs* govern themselves, through nonprofit boards, [benefit trusts](/e/long-term-benefit-trust) and [scaling policies](/e/responsible-scaling-policy); that is [industry self-governance](/e/industry-self-governance). *The funders* shape the field by deciding what research exists; Open Philanthropy's $55 million founded the Washington think tank [CSET](/e/cset). *The states* write law ([EU AI Act](/e/eu-ai-act), California's [SB 53](/e/sb-53)), rename their institutes around [security](/e/ai-security), and fight over who has authority, as in the US [executive order](/e/state-preemption-order) against state AI laws. *The courts* arbitrate, as in the 2026 [Anthropic–Pentagon dispute](/e/anthropic-pentagon-dispute). And *the public* is mostly absent.

**The question it leaves you with.** A handful of labs is building systems that their own leaders describe as potentially the most consequential technology in history. Name one decision about those systems that you, or anyone you know, has had a vote on.

**Where it goes in the story.** The [OpenAI board crisis](/e/openai-board-crisis) of 2023 showed a nonprofit board trying to use its power and losing to employees and investors in five days. The [recapitalization](/e/openai-recapitalization) of 2025 was reviewed by two state attorneys general. Summits went from "safety" at Bletchley to "action" in [Paris](/e/paris-ai-action-summit), where the US and UK refused to sign, to "impact" in [New Delhi](/e/india-ai-impact-summit). The [ustopian](/e/ustopia) alternative, deciding together through tools like [Decidim](/e/decidim), is the least funded answer of all.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Some argue that frontier AI moves too fast and is too technical for democratic deliberation, and that experts must lead. The reply: every expert in this story, from Oxford philosophers to lab CEOs to defense secretaries, is also a stakeholder with a worldview. Expertise informs a decision; it doesn't settle who should make it.
