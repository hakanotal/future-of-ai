"AI safety" sounds uncontroversial: who wants unsafe AI? But in the EA milieu it came to mean something specific and narrow: preventing a hypothetical future catastrophe in which an uncontrolled, superhumanly capable AI destroys or permanently disempowers humanity.

**The idea.** The field rests on a chain of premises. Advanced AI may be built soon ([intelligence explosion](/e/intelligence-explosion)). Almost any goal gives such a system reasons to seek power and resist shutdown ([instrumental convergence](/e/instrumental-convergence)). So we must solve [alignment](/e/ai-alignment) before we build it, or we get [existential risk](/e/existential-risk). [Effective altruism](/e/effective-altruism) supplied most of the early money and people: [Open Philanthropy](/e/open-philanthropy), the [FTX Future Fund](/e/ftx-future-fund), and a pipeline into [OpenAI](/e/openai), [DeepMind](/e/deepmind) and [Anthropic](/e/anthropic).

**The question it leaves you with.** Safe *for whom*, and *from what*? A system can be "aligned" with its owner's intentions and still help surveil a city, write disinformation or replace a workforce. Is that a safety failure or a success?

**Where it goes in the story.** The vocabulary split the field in two: AI safety (alignment, catastrophe) and [AI ethics](/e/ai-ethics) (bias, labor, environment, power). Then, from 2025, governments took the word away. The UK's AI Safety Institute became the [AI Security Institute](/e/uk-ai-security-institute); the US institute became the [Center for AI Standards and Innovation](/e/us-ai-safety-institute). The same risks, renamed as [security](/e/ai-security), acquired a new owner: the state.

**Strongest objection / best reply.** Critics like [Timnit Gebru](/e/timnit-gebru) argue that talk of speculative doom legitimizes building dangerous, unscoped systems while present harms go unseen. The reply from inside the field: the first people to take AI risk seriously were members of this community, years before anyone else, and today's evaluations for bioweapons and cyberattacks came out of their work. Both things are true, which is why the question of who decides matters so much.
